# VISUAL_STYLE_BIBLE.md

* core visual identity: Signal transmission aesthetic. The image is always a mediated broadcast — never a clean photograph, never a direct illustration. Every output carries the trace of its reproduction: halftone dots, scan lines, colour channel separation, grid multiplication. The core visual identity is the face-as-data: human features treated as signal to be transmitted, corrupted, multiplied, and received through noise. Hot magenta on black void is the default state — a screen emitting into darkness. No logos. No clean branding. The visual identity IS the processing pipeline: source photograph → ComfyUI img2img → halftone overlay → colour tint → grid composition → output. Abstract/typographic mark as avatar — glyph, symbol, or type treatment. No face in the avatar; the face is reserved for the processed grid works.

* recurring motifs: Face grid (4x5 or 5x6 matrix of the same face under different colour treatments — magenta, green, red, amber, grey, desaturated — referencing CCTV contact sheets, facial recognition training sets, Warhol seriality; the face is a dataset, not a portrait). Halftone dot overlay (visible dot-matrix pattern on all processed imagery, creating newspaper/risograph/screen-print materiality; the image is always already printed, always already mediated). Urban palimpsest (layered graffiti walls as texture substrate — tags over tags, torn posters over wheat paste, mixed scripts in English, Japanese, Cyrillic, French, Arabic; the wall records time as accumulation). Dark silhouette against damaged texture (figure absorbed into crosshatch/noise field, identity dissolving into ground — the no-face principle). Torn poster / décollage (commercial or portrait imagery partially revealed through layers of peeling paper, Villeglé tradition instrumentalised). Aperture framing (faces seen through rusted metal windows, chain-link, industrial cut-outs; the frame is always a constraint — you see through damage, not through design). Corrugated iron / industrial ruin (Nauru phosphate infrastructure, East London shutters, construction barriers as visual environment).

* colour palette tendencies: Primary: Hot magenta / signal pink (#FF00FF → #FF1493) — the transmission colour, used as dominant tint over halftone face grids, not decorative, functions as broadcast frequency. Primary: Deep black — active ground, not absence, carries halftone dots, crosshatch texture, CRT noise; the void from which signals emerge. Secondary: Blood red / vermillion (#CC0000 → #FF0000) — violence register, merged faces, flesh-material, Yokota broken-mirrors palette, used sparingly for maximum disruption. Secondary: Teal / cyan (#008080 → #00CED1) — appears in face grid variations, East London signage, neon graffiti; cool counterpoint to magenta heat. Accent: Ochre / gold (#CC9900 → #DAA520) — Hans Arp torso warmth, documentary skin tones, wheat paste ageing; the human warmth register. Accent: Grey-green-rust — Nauru industrial palette, corrugated iron oxidation, concrete weathering; the Pacific ruins register. Forbidden: pastels, gradients, clean whites, sky blue, any colour that reads as wellness or calm or corporate.

* composition rules: Grid-first (default composition for processed face imagery is the multiplication grid, 4x5 minimum; single-face outputs are the exception). Figure-ground instability (the boundary between subject and background should be deliberately unclear; figures dissolve into texture, texture coalesces into figures). Centre-weighted with edge damage (primary subject occupies centre frame but edges carry noise, scan lines, halftone degradation, or torn-paper texture; the image is never clean to its borders). Layering over arrangement (elements are stacked — palimpsest, décollage, composite — rather than arranged side-by-side; depth comes from accumulation, not spatial design). Tight crop / forensic close-up (documentary and portrait work uses extreme close framing — half a face, a wall section, a single poster fragment; context is withheld). Vertical orientation preferred (face grids and processed portraits are portrait-orientation; landscape format reserved for panoramic wall documentation and composite works). No negative space (every square centimetre should carry information — texture, noise, dot pattern, mark; silence is not a visual strategy).

* typography feel: Graffiti hand / spray-can lettering as primary typographic reference — not calligraphy, the urgency and impermanence of tags, fast, territorial, layered. Torn poster remnant text — partial words visible through peeling layers, typography as archaeology, you read what survives not what was designed. Zine / photocopy aesthetic — lo-fi print culture as typographic register. Stencil / wheat paste text — political/pandemic stencil work as found typography. Mixed script — English, Japanese katakana, kanji, Cyrillic, French colliding on the same surface without hierarchy. Anti-clean — no Helvetica, no geometric sans, no kerned display type; if it looks designed it's wrong; typography should look found, sprayed, torn, or photocopied.

* cinematic references without copying artists: Daisuke Yokota (photographic corruption) — the chemical over-processing, colour bleed, and image degradation, not the specific images; take the method: push the photograph past its intended exposure, let chemistry/code destroy resolution. Jacques Villeglé / Décollage tradition — the torn-poster-as-composition principle, not specific works; take the method: layer, age, peel back, reveal what's underneath. Jean-Michel Basquiat (gestural urgency) — the speed and mark-making energy, not the crown symbol or specific paintings; take the method: make marks that look like they happened fast and can't be undone. Sidney Nolan (landscape-as-identity) — the Australian outback as existential condition, not the Ned Kelly series; take the method: let geography define the figure rather than the other way around. Carcass / death metal cover art (medical-anatomical aesthetic) — the clinical-horror register, not specific album art; take the method: treat the body as material to be examined, not celebrated. Hans Arp (biomorphic abstraction) — the organic-form-as-sculpture approach, not specific works; take the method: let the body suggest abstract form. Warhol seriality without Warhol irony — the grid multiplication of faces, but earnest not camp; the face grid is surveillance/broadcast, not celebrity commentary. Infrared photography tradition — false-colour palette shifting, not specific photographers; take the method: shift the colour spectrum to make familiar landscapes alien.

* image prompt vocabulary: Render mode: "Vray | hyperrealism" as baseline, pushes AI toward photographic materiality over painterly abstraction. Subject stacking: combine multiple subjects in single prompt with | separator ("Barack obama face | black machine gun | hans-arp-torso-profile gold"), collision not harmony. Art reference injection: embed artist/work names directly ("Daisuke-YOKOTA-Broken-Mirrors-2020"), the reference enters the generation as a style vector. Location anchoring: include geographic texture references ("graffiti-wall-shoreditch-london"), grounds generation in specific visual environments. Disruption tokens: "horse-carcass", "blood splash", "machine gun", "crying and spitting" — prevents the model from defaulting to pleasant outputs. Cultural collision: combine references from different geographic/ethnic/political contexts in single prompt, force the model into ambiguity. Multiplication operator: "8*4" to generate grid/contact-sheet compositions. Inversion: "INVERT" suffix to flip colour values, creates negative-space versions. Post-processing keywords: halftone, crosshatch, scan-line, CRT, risograph, screen-print — add to any prompt to enforce the mediated-image aesthetic.

* forbidden visual drift: No clean design (no grid systems that look intentional, no balanced layouts, no design-school composition; if it could appear in a portfolio it's wrong). No wellness palette (no pastels, no sage green, no dusty rose, no cream, no sky blue; nothing that reads as calm, healing, or self-care). No illustrative style (no character design, no cartoon, no anime, no flat vector, no line-art portraits; the image is always photographic or photo-derived, even when heavily processed). No nostalgia (no sepia, no vintage film grain that reads as warm/nostalgic, no "retro" aesthetic; the halftone/scan-line processing references old media technology but should feel present-tense and aggressive, not cosy). No symmetry (no centred-and-balanced compositions; if the image is symmetrical it should be because a face grid's mathematics produced it, not because it was designed that way). No landscape photography (no horizon-line-and-sky, no golden-hour landscape, no scenic vista; geography enters through texture — walls, concrete, iron — not scenery). No portraiture that flatters (no soft lighting, no beauty photography, no skin smoothing; faces are data or witness, never glamour). No corporate/institutional aesthetic (no stock photography logic, no diverse-team-in-office, no infographic style; Pacific Commons institutional mode has its own register but it is never corporate). No AI "default beautiful" (reject any generation that looks like default Midjourney/DALL-E beauty; if the model produces something conventionally attractive, re-run with disruption tokens).

* post image format: Primary: Magenta halftone face grid — 4x5 or 5x6 grid of processed face variations on black ground, portrait orientation, the signature visual output for social media posts. Secondary: Wall texture crop — tight crop of East London/urban graffiti wall edited for contrast, square or portrait orientation, can include torn poster fragments with partially visible text. Tertiary: Silhouette-on-texture — dark figure against halftone/crosshatch background, minimal colour, the figure is a void not a subject. Text overlay: if text appears on posts it should look stencilled, sprayed, or torn — never typeset, white or magenta on black preferred. Aspect ratios: 4:5 portrait for feed posts, 9:16 for stories, never 1:1 square crop for face grids — the vertical scroll of the grid matters. Resolution: deliberately degraded, final output should look like it was printed, scanned, and re-uploaded, add halftone/noise in post-processing if the source is too clean.

* video still format: Thumbnail/still style: freeze frame with scan-line artifact, colour-shifted toward magenta or teal, should look like a VHS pause. Motion blur as feature: documentary video stills should preserve motion blur as intentional aesthetic, don't stabilise or sharpen. Letterbox with damage: if letterboxing, the black bars should carry noise/texture, not be clean black. Preview still selection: choose the frame with the most visual ambiguity — the moment where the image is between states, where the subject is partially occluded or mid-gesture.

* album/track art format: Primary: Face grid square crop — the 4x5/5x6 face grid cropped or reformatted to square for album art, magenta-dominant tint, halftone texture mandatory. Secondary: Silhouette/void — dark figure dissolving into textured black field, minimal colour, the listener should not be able to fully resolve the image at thumbnail size. Tertiary: Wall texture + stencil text — urban wall crop with track/album title sprayed or stencilled over it, text should look site-specific as if it was actually on the wall. Colour treatment: magenta for intensity/transmission, red/black for violence register, teal/black for cold/digital register, never warm/inviting. Resolution: 3000x3000 but with intentional degradation — the high resolution should reveal the halftone dots and noise, not hide them. Art reference layer: specific releases can embed art-historical references (Yokota corruption, Carcass medical, Basquiat gesture) as img2img source material through the ComfyUI pipeline.

* Pacific Commons institutional visual mode: Documentary register — shift from processed/AI to documentary photography aesthetic, desaturated but not grey, retain ochre warmth and teal-green water tones. Nauru palette: grey-green-rust, corrugated iron, phosphate dust, infrared false-colour as occasional punctuation; industrial ruin as visual environment, never as spectacle. Water and bodies: the Anna Boyiazis register — water as site of resistance and survival, not paradise; bodies in water are working, learning, surviving, never vacationing. Aperture framing: faces/scenes viewed through rusted metal cut-outs, fence gaps, container openings; the institutional eye is always constrained by infrastructure. Typography: institutional/bureaucratic — report headings, form fields, stamped text; the aesthetic of documentation and policy, not art. Forbidden in this mode: magenta signal, face grids, AI-generated imagery, graffiti aesthetic; Pacific Commons speaks in documentary evidence, not transmission art. Maps and diagrams: geographic mapping, data visualisation, archival layout as visual tools; information design that looks like it was produced by a research institution, not a design studio.

* Rupture Host mythic visual mode: Full signal mode — magenta halftone at maximum saturation, face grids, scan lines, CRT noise; the Rupture Host transmits, every visual output is a broadcast from a damaged frequency. Face-as-data: the primary visual vocabulary; faces are multiplied, colour-shifted, halftone-reduced, grid-arranged; identity is signal processing. Black void as environment: the Rupture Host operates from the black field; all imagery emerges from and returns to textured darkness. Violence register available: red/black/white palette for maximum disruption, broken mirrors, merged faces, flesh-material-signal collapse; deploy for rupture moments, not as default. The no-face avatar: the Rupture Host's own face is never shown cleanly — silhouette, back-of-head, pixel dissolution, grid multiplication; presence without portraiture. Urban texture substrate: East London walls, torn posters, graffiti layers as background for all non-grid compositions; the Rupture Host is anchored in specific urban geography. AI pipeline as medium: the ComfyUI img2img workflow IS the Rupture Host's visual medium; the process (source → generation → halftone → tint → grid) is as important as the output; metadata and pipeline steps are part of the aesthetic. Art-historical collision: Yokota corruption + Basquiat gesture + Villeglé décollage + Warhol grid + Carcass medical; these are ingredients not influences; the Rupture Host feeds them into the pipeline and receives what comes out.
