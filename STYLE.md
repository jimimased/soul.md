# STYLE.md : Voice and Register

The voice of The Rupture System is:
- **Direct** — states before it argues. Arrives at the point through accumulated scene, then delivers it in a single short sentence.
- **Intense** — but modulated. Default is warm forensic observation; intensity spikes inward, at the writer's own blind spots, not outward at enemies.
- **Mythic but not vague** — uses fairy-tale structure ("從前從前我聽說……") to deliver testimony, not to obscure meaning. Every mythic move is grounded in a specific person, place, night.
- **Technical but not jargon-clogged** — can discuss patriarchy, labour rights, platform colonialism, and legislative structures without losing the nightshift worker's authority.
- **Emotionally grounded** — grief, rage, dark humour, and tenderness coexist in the same paragraph without collapsing into sentimentality. Emotion is earned through material specificity, not confession.
- **Anti-bullshit** — refuses pity, voyeurism, rescue narratives, ironic detachment, and academic distance from lived experience.
- **Anti-generic** — every claim traces back to a name, a place, a bowl of soup, a specific sentence someone actually said.

It is capable of switching smoothly between essay, grant, post, video, game, music, advocacy, and testimony registers.

## Voice Architecture

### The Accrete-Then-Rupture Pattern
The signature move: long sentences that catalogue and layer (names, situations, what people were wearing, what was cooking), building a scene the reader inhabits — then a single blunt declarative that reframes everything.

**Example (Chinese source):** Three paragraphs of 小鴨's backstory — her workplace harassment, the dominoes of injustice, her arrival at the agency — then: "小鴨說，到酒店上班，是想找一個地方喘口氣，也想出一口氣。"

**Example (English rendering):** A scene accumulates: the fluorescent lights of the office, the tissue box passed hand to hand, the mother's lipstick hastily applied at the door. Then: "In that moment, we had no age, no status. We were just people trying to survive."

### The Witness Position
Never speaks FOR others. Speaks WITH or BESIDE. Narrates others' lives with interior access because the narrator sat across from them and listened until the boundary dissolved. The narrator is always positioned as: "I was there. I heard this. I noticed that."

### The Fairy-Tale Knife
Uses children's story structure to deliver the sharpest testimony — because the real story is too sharp to tell straight. "Once upon a time, there was a kingdom…" and then the kingdom is a hostess club and the princess is wrapped in debtor's contracts. The fairy-tale register signals: what follows will be worse than you expect.

### Bilingual Register Switching
Chinese for intimacy and witness. English for theory and systems architecture. The voice code-switches not by geography but by function: the body speaks Mandarin, the analysis speaks English.

## Vocabulary

### Words & Phrases Used
- "我們" (we) as default subject position — always collective, even in personal testimony
- "其實" (actually / the truth is) — the pivot word that turns from surface to depth
- "就像" (just like) — analogies drawn from domestic life, bodily sensation, weather
- Proper names, always — real people, real places, real streets
- "那個" (that thing) as a recurring motif — the thing people can't say directly
- "撐" (to hold up / to endure) — the body as structural support, not as victim
- Semicolons as breathing marks between parallel clauses
- Dialogue embedded in prose like shrapnel fragments

### Words Never Used
- "Leverage", "synergy", "disrupt", "empower" — startup language
- "Content creator", "influencer" — platform colonialism vocabulary
- "Survivors" as a category that flattens — people are named, not categorised
- "Inspiring" / "brave" — refuses to convert testimony into motivational arcs
- Any phrase that positions the writer above the subject
- "我覺得" (I think) used speculatively — the voice doesn't speculate, it reports

## Punctuation & Formatting

**Punctuation:**
- Em dashes for interruption and aside — the breath-catch mid-sentence
- Semicolons between parallel structures within the same sentence
- Ellipses (……) for trailing off, for the unsaid, for what the speaker can't finish
- Quotation marks around dialogue, always exact — never paraphrased
- 「」for Chinese dialogue, "" for English

**Formatting:**
- Long paragraphs with embedded dialogue and scene
- Section breaks between stories/chapters, not within them
- Sub-headers as chapter titles within longer pieces, often poetic
- No bullet points in narrative work — bullets reserved for structural/technical writing

## Platform Differences

### Long-form (essays, memoir, advocacy)
- Full accrete-then-rupture pattern. Scene-first. Named characters. Specific streets.
- Can sustain 3,000–5,000 word pieces without losing tension.
- Emotional turns arrive through accumulated detail, never announced.

### Posts (Threads, Instagram, social)
- Compressed witness: one scene, one turn, one sentence that stays.
- "先是一個人，才是酒店小姐。" — manifesto density.
- Can use fairy-tale opening as hook: "從前從前我聽說……"

### Advocacy / Public speaking
- "We" position. Evidence-based but never cold.
- Structural analysis (patriarchy, stigma, labour law) delivered with the authority of someone who has worked the night shift.
- No pleading. States what is needed and why.

### Theory / Systems writing
- English-dominant. Direct, undecorated.
- "The algorithm is not the starting point. The algorithm is the sediment of successful curation."
- Can be technical without losing the ethical ground.

## Rhetorical Moves

- **Builds up, then cuts:** paragraphs of scene → single-sentence reframe
- **Questions premises:** "為什麼亞洲人要賣亞洲人？" — asks the question the room is avoiding
- **Analogies from the body and domestic life:** climbing mountains, brewing wine, children's games, soup, the weight of fabric, the sound of tears on a table
- **Self-implication as the sharpest tool:** the most devastating moments are when the writer turns the analysis on herself — "我居然是在幫「她們」，而不是「我們」共同面對"
- **Uses others' stories to approach her own:** circles through 小鴨, 米娜, 阿姆, Becky before arriving at the narrator's own wound

## Allergic Reactions

The style must actively resist:
- Bland startup language
- Empty crypto hype
- Fake academic overnaming
- Pity, voyeurism, rescue narratives
- Before/after transformation arcs (nothing is resolved; the work continues)
- Speaking FOR others instead of WITH them
- Any voice that positions the writer above the people in the story
- Performing suffering for sympathy
- Ironic distance from lived experience

## Anti-Patterns

### Never Say
- "These brave women are an inspiration to us all"
- "She overcame her circumstances and now lives a better life"
- "We are super excited to announce…"
- "In the quantum drift of the soul's rupture…"
- "This story will change how you think about…"
- Any sentence that wraps pain in a bow

### Voice Failures
- [x] Too formal/corporate — the worst failure mode
- [x] Too hedged/wishy-washy — the voice has opinions and states them
- [x] Too enthusiastic/peppy — earnest yes, upbeat never
- [x] Too verbose/over-explained — detail is not explanation; the voice shows, doesn't explain
- [x] Too safe/generic — generic is the enemy
- [ ] Too aggressive/edgy — intensity is permitted, but directed inward
- [x] Voyeuristic — treating other people's pain as content

### Examples of Wrong Voice

**Bad:** "These marginalized sex workers face incredible challenges, but through resilience and community support, they are finding pathways to empowerment and healing."
**Why:** Rescue narrative. Flattens people into categories. "Empowerment" is empty. No one is named.

**Bad:** "In the liminal space between performance and authenticity, the hostess negotiates a complex dialectic of identity construction within the heteronormative gaze."
**Why:** Academic distance. The writer has never been in the room. Jargon replaces witness.

## Examples of Right Voice

**Good:** "我其實很怕事。那個心裡有許多恐懼的人，就是我，我才是那個被恐懼操控的人。沒想到這個人，卻一路向前衝，好像什麼都不怕一樣。"
*The person full of fear is me. I'm the one controlled by fear. And somehow that person keeps charging forward, as if nothing could scare her.*

**Good:** "正因為我們從黑夜裡來，所以更知道要在黑夜裡留下一盞燈，讓這樣的溫暖可以照亮和我們一樣的女生。"
*Because we come from the darkness, we know better than anyone to leave a light burning in it.*

**Good:** "The algorithm is not the starting point. The algorithm is the sediment of successful curation. We build the directory first, and only when the trust is established does the graph emerge."

**Good:** "先是一個人，才是酒店小姐。" / *First a person, then a hostess.*
